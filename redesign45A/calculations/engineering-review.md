## Conduction and thermal requirements

For two matched parallel devices per bank, Rbank=Rdevice/2, Pbank=I²Rbank, Pdevice=(I/2)²Rdevice and Vdrop=I(Rfront+Rdownstream). The ideal current is 22.5 A per MOSFET. Manufacturer RDS(on) values below are quoted at specified gate voltage, junction temperature and test current (25 A); they are screening inputs, not a solved steady-state model at every load and temperature.

**Every row requires at least 10 V at each physical front MOSFET gate relative to its source. This minimum has not been established for the circuit.** TI charge-pump/VCAP thresholds are not a guaranteed minimum loaded GATE-to-SOURCE output. The front datasheet has no 4.5 V RDS(on) maximum on which to base an alternative guaranteed loss. The downstream values use its 4.5 V characterization, rather than assume its 10 V values. The 25 °C row cannot describe equilibrium at 25 °C ambient with nonzero loss. Mixed hot rows use different temperatures at each bank and are not a universal maximum-loss bound. [TI electrical characteristics](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm74500-q1.pdf), [MOSFET limits](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PSMN1R3-80SSF.pdf), [downstream limits](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PSMN2R0-55YLH.pdf).

The common-board column divides the entire available temperature rise by 7.18875 W and leaves **no** allowance for junction-to-board rise, copper/connector dissipation or other heat. It is an optimistic screening budget, not a validated board thermal resistance. Real devices heat each other: Tji=Ta+ΣθijPj. Four copper layers do not create four independent cooling surfaces. The Nexperia downstream 42 °C/W example uses a specified 25.4 mm square, 70 µm copper test board; that example does not meet the illustrative 26.20 °C/W single-device budget at 60 °C ambient, and cannot be assigned independently to each device on this small shared PCB. Its junction-to-mounting-base maximum 0.45 °C/W (front 0.3 °C/W) is not junction-to-ambient. [Manufacturer thermal conditions](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PSMN2R0-55YLH.pdf), [front conditions](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PSMN1R3-80SSF.pdf).

An illustrative 60/40 current split means 27/18 A, not 22.5/22.5 A. At 2.2/4.9 mΩ the hot-side front device dissipates 1.6038 W and hot-side downstream 3.5721 W; each is 44% above the equal-sharing device loss. Total bank loss rises 4%. The illustrative downstream thermal budget at 60 °C ambient /125 °C target then tightens to 18.20 °C/W. Positive fully-enhanced RDS(on) temperature coefficient helps static sharing, but does not guarantee dynamic gate/diode sharing or cancellation of copper/contact asymmetry. One parallel device open leaves 45 A in the survivor: 4.455 W front or 9.9225 W downstream at those resistance points; this is not a fail-safe rating.

Retaining four MOSFETs is reasonable for a placement study because the user requires preservation. It is not an approval to operate continuously at 45 A in an unspecified enclosure. A measured/validated cooling solution, actual minimum gate drive, shared-board thermal model and current-path temperatures determine whether the unchanged four devices can be released. Loss scales as I²: 45 A loss is 56.25% of an otherwise identical 60 A screen, not a separate qualification.

## Gate drive, local components and placement priorities

The front controller specifies charge-pump current at a particular VCAP−SOURCE test point, and its separate peak gate-source current is not the continuous pump current. Two front MOSFETs have total maximum Ciss 33.294 nF at the datasheet test point; TI's ≥10× rule requires at least approximately 333 nF **effective** C2. The nominal 1 µF X7R part must retain that value after tolerance, bias, temperature and aging. This retention has not been verified from a Murata bias curve. The capacitor sees the boosted VCAP−SOURCE voltage, not the full 25.2 V rail in steady normal operation. The illustrative nominal enable delay 6.5×1 µF/300 µA +75 µs is about 21.74 ms; it is not a full-10-V gate-enhancement guarantee. [TI capacitor and startup guidance](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm74500-q1.pdf).

For the two downstream devices, Qg at 10 V is 238 nC typical /368 nC maximum at the manufacturer test point. At a hypothetical 10 V, two external 1 MΩ bleeds consume 20 µA and the NCV's typical 1.1 MΩ internal G−S resistor another 9.09 µA. Using its initial minimum 170 µA pump current at the stated datasheet test point gives 368 nC/(170−29.09) µA ≈2.61 ms. This is a sizing illustration, **not guaranteed turn-on time**: Qg, pump current and internal resistance vary through the trajectory, and device/harness/load conditions differ from those tests. The 10 ohm gate resistors belong beside each physical gate; they support damping and do not impose a controlled output-voltage ramp. C5 remains DNP. DNP D2/D3 cannot be credited as protection; the nominal 15 V BZT52H-C15 can be 15.6 V at its reference current, above the TI 15 V absolute gate-to-source limit before transient dynamic impedance. [NCV characteristics](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/ncv68261-d.pdf), [optional zener](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/BZT52H_SER.pdf).

Keep both MOSFET banks on the primary current side as a first placement candidate, arrange balanced source/drain copper for each parallel pair, and avoid narrow MID bottlenecks. Move small parts bottom only if the resulting complete loops remain short and the mechanical envelope permits it. C1 belongs near U2 SOURCE/GND; C2 near U2 VCAP/SOURCE; C3 near U1 IN/GND. R14 must sense the MOSFET source locally without load-current shared resistance. Gate resistors stay at their gates; gate-source bleeds remain local. C4 belongs close to the downstream output reference. A capacitor directly opposite its controller can work with short paired vias, but the via and return-loop inductance must be reviewed during routing. Do not trade controlled local gate/Kelvin geometry for a lower top-side occupancy percentage.

R13 remains **10 kΩ**, with no electrical-value change. At MID=25.2 V the nominal power is 25.2²/10,000=**63.504 mW**; at the −1% resistance limit (9,900 Ω) it is **64.145 mW**. This is a conservative rail-voltage calculation because the normal front-bank drop reduces MID slightly. The supplied proposed-placement update locates R13 on the front at (127,105) mm, near hot board/terminal regions. The exact BOM manufacturer part, its rated-power conditions, temperature derating and actual local component temperature must be verified before assigning a power margin. This audit has not established a manufacturer power or derating limit and does not infer one from the 0805 size. Keep the value unchanged while assessing cooling and placement.

The proposed placement also puts C4 on the back under/near Q4's drain region: verify its actual thermal exposure and effective capacitance at temperature after copper heat spreading and stackup are defined. The controller bodies clear the projected principal front drain regions in the proposed placement, but backside gate resistors still lie near heated regions. Projected geometric separation is not thermal isolation through a multilayer board. Review local temperatures, heat-spreading planes, via coupling and ambient/cooling after routing; placement alone cannot validate these temperatures.

## Complete current path and output branches

The main positive path is TP9 → Q1/Q2 → MID → Q3/Q4 → VOUT → every output. Every output's full return also needs a rated path through GND → TP8. The new J4/J5 VOUT/GND terminations are additional outputs on these same nodes: the sum of all discharge branch currents is at most the requested **45 A total**. They do not create independently protected branches, branch current limits or a 45 A rating per connector. A rated new output cannot upgrade the unidentified incoming solder termination or an old four-pin connection.

The preserved J2/J3 four-pin pad groups have 1.5 mm pads /1.0 mm drills; ideal 45 A sharing would be 11.25 A per pin, or 15 A per remaining pin after one open. The unknown connector contact, solder, pin, mating half and cable must be rated as an assembly. TP8/TP9 have 2.5 mm pads /2 mm drills and each carries the common 45 A path. Nominal 0.25 mm rings describe geometry, not ampacity or residual-ring acceptance. A real extra 1 mΩ anywhere in the common loop adds 45 mV and 2.025 W at 45 A. Ground must be assessed as a power rail, not only as a controller reference plane.

The accompanying copper and barrel CSVs use only R=ρL/A with explicitly assumed dimensions, copper and plating. They select no trace, plane, via count or stackup and are **not Saturn, IPC-2152, IPC-2221 ampacity or temperature-rise results**. Parallel-path models assume equal sharing and omit neckdowns, thermal reliefs, solder joints, pin injection, mutual heating and spreading resistance. Via barrels must not be credited with a guessed current rating. Previous candidate-corridor dimensions belong to the old placement study and cannot be copied as a newly routed design. No actual Saturn result is available.

## Fault protection and the balance-function conflict

The upstream LM74500 stage is needed in the preserved architecture because the NCV IN/S DC absolute minimum is −18 V, whereas a reversed fully charged pack presents −25.2 V. The front controller's negative-input range and 80 V MOSFETs make a static reverse-battery candidate. A charged +25.2 V output together with −25.2 V raw input can impose up to about 50.4 V across a blocking front device in a credible charged-mid/backfeed state; transients and cold breakdown margin require verification. D1 is oriented anode GND/cathode MID to clamp negative MID. Its 60 V/3 A part label is not proof of the transient clamp energy, pulse current or NCV pin excursions. Hot reversal, harness inductance, diode recovery, gate discharge and connected charger/monitor conditions remain unqualified. [TI limits](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/lm74500-q1.pdf), [NCV limits](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/ncv68261-d.pdf), [MOSFET limits](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/data-sheet/PSMN1R3-80SSF.pdf).

Both MOSFET banks are oriented for forward body-diode conduction before the charge pumps enhance them. This circuit has no controlled inrush limit. EN-off is not positive supply isolation; no overcurrent/short-circuit limiter, qualified thermal shutdown, UV disconnect or reverse-current blocking has been added. With NCV D grounded it is in RPP mode; this is not its drain-sensing ideal-diode reverse-current-blocking mode. Parallel MOSFETs also do not make the circuit single-fault tolerant: one front drain-source short bypasses that bank's protection. Four devices should not be presented as four independent safety barriers.

Load capacitance/ESR, source impedance, cable inductance, load turn-on, external charger and reversal timing are unknown, so inrush peak, body-diode energy, MOSFET linear/avalanche SOA and dynamic negative-MID clamp stresses cannot be calculated to a release result. The selected nominal 1 µF output capacitor alone stores 0.318 mJ at 25.2 V. A hypothetical external 1 mF stores 0.318 J and 10 mF 3.175 J; these are scale examples, not a selected load. Cold/aged capacitor effective values and surge source current matter as well.

**The current circuit does not implement functional balance-connector reverse-polarity protection. J1 and TP1..TP7 are deliberately open.** They are not a protected seven-wire interface and cannot convey cell taps to a BMS in this state. The new requirement to preserve all existing electrical connections while calling the board a balance-reversal protector is therefore a functional conflict that placement cannot resolve. Connecting those nodes directly would silently change the circuit and would not solve reversal. If the external receiver ties a raw balance G lead to board GND while the main leads are reversed, a pack short can bypass the main protection through the balance cable. Intermediate taps or B6 can also back-power an attached monitor. The acceptable topology depends on receiver absolute pin limits, input architecture, balancing/charging currents, wire sequence and hot-plug permutations, which remain unknown. The preserved open interface must be treated as unimplemented; it is not evidence of the requested operating functionality.

## Decisions for this placement phase and before release

Proceed now with the reversible two-sided placement candidate, unchanged 36-part values/nodes, clearly identified new output nodes, complete positive/return corridor reservations, component-side comparison, bottom-move list and current native ERC/DRC. Preserve the fixed outline and original connector/pad geometry. Stop before routing until the requested placement approval arrives. An unrouted-board unconnected count is expected, but footprint clearance errors must remain visible and must not be hidden through exclusions.

Before 45 A release, establish duty/duration, ambient/enclosure/cooling, actual input/output contact and cable terminations, branch allocation, load/charger/BMS data, reversal/hot-plug cases and the actual balance-protection specification. Then approve one fabricator stackup/process including minimum finished copper/plating, local 0.195 mm NCV EP spacing, 0.200 mm DDF8 spacing, preserved 0.250 mm nominal PTH rings, mask/stencil and assembly. Verify minimum physical gate enhancement, effective decoupling, actual current sharing and end-to-end temperature rise under those conditions. Route only after placement approval, run native ERC/DRC and manufacturing checks on the routed artifact, and test startup, load, reverse and hot-plug stresses using a controlled test plan. These items determine whether the preserved circuit is acceptable or needs a separately authorized electrical change.
